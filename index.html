One colour, told by the sky

Simulator · civil time zones enabled · astronomy fixture-validated vs JPL reference (tests/)

The orb has no hands and no numbers — just a single glowing colour. It warms as the sun climbs, peaks at solar noon, cools through dusk, and at night it answers to the moon instead: indigo silver-blue while the moon is up, deepest navy when it isn't.

The edge of the face is a 24-hour dial: top = midnight, right = 6 AM, bottom = noon, left = 6 PM. The Hairline Halo — on the edge itself (the default): a hairline that is part of the perimeter — gold from sunrise to sunset, pale silver just inside it from moonrise to moonset. Its ends are the events — no separate marks. The black dot straddling the edge is the current time. Switch styles under Event marks in Controls to compare.

On blends four seasonal peak tints through the year — spring blush, summer amber-red, fall russet, winter pale gold. Off keeps the plain warmth scale: deep amber summer peak, pale straw winter peak.

The default is the Hairline Halo — on the edge itself: the gold segment is the rim between sunrise and sunset, so there's only ever one circle. Compare it with the Offset halo, which floats the same daylight / moonlight arcs just outside the orb — the orb stays untouched, but there are two silhouettes. Horizon drops the circle entirely: one line through the middle, events as tiny dots along it.

How to read it: scrub the time slider and the orb follows the sun's arc for that exact date and place — long amber evenings in June, a brief pale-gold midday in December. Press Play day to watch a full 24 hours run. At night the sun hands over to the moon: drag to just after sunset on a full-moon date and the orb lifts into indigo silver-blue as the moon rises; on a new-moon night, or after moonset, it settles to its dimmest navy. Sun and moon positions are computed in your browser from standard astronomical formulas (truncated series, validated against a JPL ephemeris reference to within ~0.04° altitude and ~½ minute of rise/set; see tests/ in the repository).

Play whole year (under the header) runs one full midnight-to-midnight day in 4.8 seconds per day, so the orb cycles through sunrise, that day's seasonal noon peak, sunset and the moonlit night for every day of the year. Use the year slider directly underneath it to skip ahead — drag to any month and playback continues from there — while the peak tint, day length and the event marks for sunrise, sunset, moonrise and moonset drift with the seasons. Use the Event marks pulldown in Controls to compare the Hairline Halo on the edge itself (the default), the offset halo, the Horizon chord, the previous edge semicircles, or no marks at all. Collapse the Controls, Sun and Moon tiles with their Collapse buttons to keep just the orb on screen.

Using a downloaded copy on your phone: use the Download standalone HTML button at the top of this page to save this one self-contained file, then open the saved copy in Safari (in Files, tap Share → Open in Safari), not the Files preview — the preview doesn't run the page's controls, which is why the city picker and sliders appear dead there. Once it's open in Safari it works fully offline, with the Hairline Halo on the edge itself as the default event-mark style.
